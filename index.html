02

One ratio. Two very different interpretations.

  1. Validate the ledger first

    Exclude unfinished rounds and keep the scope consistent. Each row needs a strictly positive stake and a gross payout of zero or more. Negative adjustments need separate reconciliation, not a pretend negative spin.

  2. Sum the amounts before dividing

    Observed RTP = total gross payouts / total stakes × 100. Do not average per-round RTP percentages when stakes differ. A sample with no stakes has no defined RTP.

  3. Separate observation from the theoretical model

    Observed RTP describes your supplied sample. Theoretical RTP describes a game model over repeated play. The sample alone does not identify variance, a confidence interval or the chance of the next outcome.

20 ≠ 100

A two-row example catches the mistake.

A 1-unit stake pays 2, while a 9-unit stake pays 0. Averaging 200% and 0% gives 100%, but ignores the larger stake. The correct ratio is 2 units returned from 10 staked: 20%.

03

A useful result needs an honest boundary.

What to check in a real export

  • All rows use the same monetary unit and a consistent gross-payout definition.
  • Every settled round appears once; re-imported rows have not been duplicated.
  • Jackpots, bonus rounds and free stakes follow one documented accounting convention.
  • The relevant game configuration and time window are known outside this worksheet.

What this worksheet deliberately leaves open

A small ledger can show an arithmetic error. It cannot prove fairness or predict recovery after a losing period. There is no “due win” signal here and no invented confidence band based on a binomial model.

Method reference: UK Gambling Commission: how to calculate RTP. Our two-row example and validation worksheet are independent educational material.

04

RTP questions

Why not average each round’s payout percentage?

Because stakes may differ. A 1-unit stake returning 2 and a 9-unit stake returning 0 produce an overall RTP of 2/10 = 20%. The unweighted average of 200% and 0% would incorrectly report 100%.

Can observed RTP exceed 100%?

Yes. A sample can return more than was staked. That is an observed sample result and does not prove a game has a theoretical return above 100%.

Does a low observed RTP prove a fault?

No. Sample size, payout variance, completeness and the applicable rules matter. This worksheet reports arithmetic only; it does not certify a game or estimate its underlying probability distribution.

Do payouts include returned stakes?

Use gross payouts, including any returned stake where applicable. Mixing gross payouts and profit-only figures creates inconsistent results. Do not add deposits or withdrawals to this ledger.

What CSV format is accepted?

The exact header is stake,payout followed by one positive stake and one non-negative gross payout per row. Use a period as the decimal separator and commas between fields. Quotes around fields are supported. Up to 20,000 rows and 1 MB of text.

Where is the CSV processed?

Entirely in the browser. There is no file upload, account, analytics or transfer of your ledger. Do not include names or account identifiers; the tool only needs the two numeric columns.
